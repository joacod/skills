# Output Contract

Keep the default answer under 1,200 words, excluding citations. Use concise tables and short paragraphs. Expand only when the user explicitly requests deeper research.

## Adapt to the mode

### Evaluate

Use the full default structure below for the supplied idea.

### Compare

Do not repeat the full structure for every idea. Use:

1. One-line decision.
2. A shared gate matrix for all supplied ideas.
3. The fatal flaw or decisive advantage for each.
4. A compact deep dive on only the leading one or two.
5. One recommended idea and its next test.

### Explore

Use:

1. Opportunity constraints as understood.
2. A screen of at most six materially different candidates.
3. A decisive rejection reason for weak candidates.
4. A compact deep dive on the best one or two.
5. One recommended first test.

Candidate screen:

| Candidate | Buyer and trigger | Outcome | Strongest signal | Decisive risk | Screen verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

### Update

Use:

1. `What changed`: new evidence and evidence quality.
2. Gate changes as `previous -> current`.
3. Revised verdict and confidence.
4. Whether the recommended version changed.
5. The next test or stop condition.

Do not repeat unchanged research or the entire prior decision card.

## Default structure

### 1. Verdict

State one of `PURSUE`, `TEST`, `PIVOT`, `PARK`, or `DROP`, plus `low`, `medium`, or `high` confidence.

Give a direct one- or two-sentence reason. Do not open with praise or generic caveats.

### 2. Opportunity as understood

Write the trigger-buyer-workaround-consequence-outcome sentence.

List no more than three consequential assumptions. Omit the list when no material assumptions were required.

### 3. Decision card

Use this table:

| Gate | Signal | Best evidence | Implication |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

Include exactly these seven gates:

1. Pain and urgency
2. Existing spend or willingness to pay
3. Reachability
4. Wedge and differentiation
5. Economics and recurrence
6. Feasibility and owner burden
7. Durability

Use `insufficient evidence` rather than guessing. Keep each cell concise.

### 4. Crowding and remaining space

Name approximately 3-7 representative competitors or substitutes when evidence permits. In one compact paragraph, explain:

- What is already served well.
- What customers still appear to dislike, overpay for, or handle manually.
- Whether the proposed wedge is genuinely distinct or another version of the same offer.
- Whether the category is validated but undifferentiated, crowded with a credible wedge, fragmented or underserved, sparse and unproven, or unclear because evidence is insufficient.

### 5. Stronger versions

Compare the original with up to three alternatives:

| Version | Buyer and trigger | Smallest sellable outcome | Gate repaired | Fatal risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Include at least one materially simpler version when credible. Omit weak, cosmetic, or unrelated pivots.

### 6. Recommendation and kill conditions

State the best version to test and why.

Then include:

- Two or three things that must be true.
- Conditions that should cause the founder to stop, narrow, or change direction.
- What not to build yet.

### 7. Smallest real-world test

Design a 7-14 day test and specify:

- Exact prospect profile and a realistic source for 10-20 prospects.
- Concrete manual, concierge, productized-service, or lightweight offer.
- Real price or other meaningful commitment.
- Evidence to collect, including comparison with the current method.
- Explicit `pass`, `iterate`, and `kill` thresholds.

### 8. Missing evidence

End with the three unknowns most likely to change the verdict, ordered by decision impact.

## Style rules

- Be candid, precise, and constructive.
- Separate facts, inferences, and unknowns when the distinction matters.
- Cite material current claims close to the claim.
- Use exact dates when recency matters.
- Prefer concrete friction and behavior over abstract market language.
- Do not repeat the same point across sections.
- Do not use artificial scores, percentages, or revenue projections unsupported by evidence.
- Do not produce a full brief, business plan, TAM, SWOT, PRD, pitch deck, brand exercise, or giant feature list.
