# Iteration Loop

## Purpose

Update the decision when the user brings real-world evidence. Treat the skill as a learning loop rather than a one-time report.

## Evidence priority

Give more weight to costly behavior than stated opinion:

1. Payment, deposit, signed pilot, contract, repeated paid use, renewal, or expansion.
2. Access to real data, a live workflow, a scheduled implementation, or a referral to the budget owner.
3. Repeated use, completion, retention, or a measurable result in a real workflow.
4. Observed behavior and specific objections from qualified prospects.
5. Detailed interviews with buyers or users who recently experienced the trigger.
6. Stated interest, survey responses, compliments, waitlists, clicks, or generic feedback.

Do not let a large volume of weak signals outweigh a small amount of strong behavior.

## Update workflow

1. Restate the prior verdict and most dangerous assumption when available.
2. Normalize the new evidence: who acted, what they did, under which trigger, at what price, and compared with which alternative.
3. Map each observation to the affected claim and decision gate.
4. Mark whether the evidence supports, contradicts, or fails to test the claim.
5. Update only the affected gate classifications and confidence.
6. Determine whether the result reflects the idea, buyer, workflow, offer, price, scope, timing, or test design.
7. Revise the verdict, strongest version, and next test only where justified.

## Diagnose failures carefully

Do not invalidate an idea merely because one validation attempt failed. Check:

- Were prospects qualified and close to the trigger?
- Did the test involve the buyer, the user, or someone outside the actual decision?
- Was the offered outcome concrete and valuable?
- Did the price create a meaningful decision?
- Was the trust proof sufficient?
- Did the test deliver the outcome or only describe future software?
- Was the sample large enough to expose a pattern?

Also do not excuse repeated rejection by endlessly changing presentation. When qualified buyers understand the offer and still will not commit, lower the relevant pain, spend, buyer clarity, or wedge gate.

## Update output

Start with `What changed`, containing:

- New evidence and its quality.
- Gates changed as `previous → current`.
- Revised verdict and confidence.

Then provide only:

- Updated decision-card rows.
- Current strongest version.
- Next test with pass, iterate, and kill thresholds.
- Evidence that would reverse the new conclusion.

Do not repeat the full original report unless the user asks.
