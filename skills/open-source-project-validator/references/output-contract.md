# Output Contract

Keep the default answer under 1,200 words, excluding citations. Expand only when the user requests deeper research.

## Adapt to the mode

For `Compare`, use one shared gate matrix, deepen only the leading one or two projects, and recommend one allocation of effort. For `Explore`, show the success constraints, screen at most four candidates, deepen the best one or two, and permit no recommendation. For `Update`, lead with new evidence, show changed gates as `previous -> current`, and do not repeat unchanged research.

## Default structure

### 1. Verdict

State `PURSUE`, `TEST`, `PIVOT`, `PARK`, or `DROP`, plus `low`, `medium`, or `high` confidence. Name the primary success objective and give a direct one- or two-sentence reason.

Keep the verdict scoped to open-source strategic value. If the user mentions revenue, include it only as supported or speculative optionality. Never imply that open-source adoption validates willingness to pay or that weak monetization invalidates strategic value.

### 2. Success contract

State:

- Primary objective and strategic audience.
- Evidence that would count as success.
- Time horizon and maintenance budget.
- Any consequential assumptions.

### 3. Project as understood

Write the user-workflow-alternative-utility-open-source-mechanism-success sentence from `workflow.md`.

### 4. Decision card

| Gate | Signal | Best evidence | Implication |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

Include exactly these seven gates:

1. User problem and utility
2. Open-source fit
3. Distinctiveness and ecosystem gap
4. Artifact and skill signal
5. Reachability and adoption path
6. Maintenance sustainability
7. Strategic leverage and optionality

Use `insufficient evidence` rather than guessing.

### 5. Landscape and remaining space

Name approximately 3-7 representative open-source projects, commercial products, platform features, internal approaches, or DIY substitutes when evidence permits. Explain what is served well, what gap remains, why open source matters, and whether the proposed position is distinct.

### 6. Stronger positions

Compare the original with up to three alternatives:

| Position | User and workflow | Smallest useful artifact | Gate repaired | Fatal risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Include a materially narrower version when credible. Omit cosmetic variants.

### 7. Recommendation and limits

State the best position and why. Include:

- Two or three things that must be true.
- Stop, narrow, or reposition conditions.
- What not to build or polish yet.
- Maximum initial and recurring maintenance commitment.

### 8. Smallest evidence-producing test

Specify the target users, source for 10-20 relevant people or teams, smallest artifact, real workflow, requested meaningful behavior, evidence to collect, and explicit `pass`, `iterate`, and `kill` thresholds.

If the primary success contract is already satisfied and no material uncertainty affects a preserve-or-park decision, replace the test with a maintenance checkpoint. Present a test for a new objective only when the user explicitly wants to pursue it, and label it separately.

### 9. Opportunity map

Briefly separate supported opportunities from speculative options. Possible paths include employment, collaboration, consulting, support, managed hosting, enterprise features, sponsorship, or licensing. Do not produce a monetization plan or imply that optionality is validated.

### 10. Missing evidence

End with the three unknowns most likely to change the verdict, ordered by decision impact.

## Style rules

- Be candid, precise, and constructive.
- Separate utility, attention, adoption, reliance, contribution, credibility, and opportunity.
- Cite material current claims close to the claim.
- Use exact dates when recency matters.
- Do not repeat the same point across sections.
- Do not use artificial aggregate scores or unsupported conversion projections.
- Do not turn the answer into a build roadmap, launch playbook, or generic open-source checklist.
