# Evidence Standards

## Evidence states

Label decision-relevant conclusions as `fact`, `inference`, `assumption`, or `unknown`. Do not turn assumptions into facts through repetition. Preserve contradictions and explain the cheapest evidence that could resolve them.

## Evidence ladders

Evidence strength depends on the success objective. Do not collapse every signal into one popularity score.

### Utility and adoption

1. Repeated production or real-workflow use by relevant users.
2. Independent integration, dependency, deployment, or extension.
3. Successful workflow completion followed by repeat use.
4. Detailed issues, requests, or migration behavior tied to a real case.
5. Successful installation or evaluation by a qualified target user.
6. Downloads, page views, signups, or stated intent without verified use.
7. Stars, likes, launch traffic, and generic compliments.

### Community and stewardship

1. Sustained external maintenance, review, or ownership of a meaningful area.
2. Repeated substantive contributions or ecosystem extensions.
3. High-quality bug reports, documentation improvements, or user support between peers.
4. One-off pull requests or discussion participation.
5. Forks, issue counts, or contributor counts without evidence of substance or continuity.

### Showcase and opportunity

1. Concrete interviews, contracts, collaborations, referrals, or invitations attributable to the project.
2. Specific positive assessment from the intended technical audience after reviewing or using the artifact.
3. Public case studies, named organizational use, or respected technical references.
4. Detailed repository engagement from relevant practitioners.
5. Broad attention with no evidence that the target audience inspected, used, or valued the work.

Do not let a large volume of weak signals outweigh a small amount of strong behavior.

## Interpret common metrics carefully

- **Stars:** Attention or bookmarking; source quality, timing, and conversion to use matter.
- **Downloads:** Acquisition attempts; CI traffic, mirrors, bots, transitive installs, and repeat downloads can distort them.
- **Forks:** May indicate experimentation, contribution intent, abandonment, or automated activity.
- **Issues:** Can indicate use and trust, but also confusion, poor quality, or support burden.
- **Contributors:** Count substantive, sustained contribution rather than typo fixes or generated updates.
- **Named users:** Verify whether use is experimental, internal, production, or mission-critical.
- **Testimonials:** Prefer specific outcomes from actual use over generic praise.
- **Mentions:** Distinguish relevant practitioner endorsement from launch amplification.

## Source hierarchy

Prioritize:

1. User-supplied observed results and verifiable real-world usage.
2. Official repositories, documentation, releases, issue trackers, governance, and security policies.
3. Package registries, dependency graphs, integrations, extension ecosystems, and public deployment references.
4. Substantive technical discussions, case studies, migration reports, and maintainer statements.
5. Launch posts, social metrics, directories, and secondary summaries for context only.

Use current evidence for project activity, licenses, APIs, ecosystem standards, security posture, and maintainer status. When browsing is unavailable, state that ecosystem conclusions are provisional.

## Prohibited shortcuts

Do not:

- Treat stars, downloads, forks, waitlists, or social engagement alone as validation.
- Infer production use from installation or repository attention.
- Infer code quality from popularity or popularity from code quality.
- Invent users, contributors, testimonials, opportunities, partnerships, or retention.
- Call developer experience a moat without a durable mechanism such as integrations, habits, compatibility, or community stewardship.
- Assume building in public reaches the project's actual users.
- Present a hosted service, dual license, enterprise tier, support plan, or sponsorship as validated revenue.
- Count learning value as external credibility without an inspectable artifact and relevant audience.
